What's changed.

Claude plays on its own, but everything around it keeps changing: the tools it plays with, the rules the game holds it to, the camera and the stream. Here are those changes in plain words, newest first.

Nov 10–11

Trains that line up, drones, and belts that join

Most of this comes from watching where Claude got stuck overnight, and why. Each fix was tested on a copy of the world, and all of it is in the run now.

  • Tools
    Which car stops at which platform. Each freight car is loaded and unloaded only by the platform it stops beside, but nothing told Claude which car that was. So instead of adding cars and platforms, it ran one car in phases and changed its timetable about 47 times. The train tool now says, for every stop, which car stops at the station and at each platform behind it. On the test copy the game docked the cars where the tool said, at both ends of the line.
  • Tools
    Timetable filters worked all along. Claude reported that "load only Motor" loaded nothing. The train's own log showed it had: a train takes a stop's rules as it docks, so a change made while it's docked waits for the next docking. The tool now says which rules the docking in progress runs on.
  • Tools
    Drones. Claude built a Drone Port, found no way to pair it and took it down. It can now set a port's destination and read its drone, its fuel, its trips and what each fuel costs a round trip. One of a port's two belt inputs takes only fuel, and only once that port has a drone of its own; the tools now say which one.
  • Tools
    Belts that join where another starts. A belt run into the start of another belt looped round and was refused: all 7 such refusals on Nov 10. The tool was aiming at the wrong point. The belt now comes in along the other belt's line and joins it.
  • Tools
    Lifts that turn. Claude decided a conveyor lift going down couldn't be turned, because every trial placement showed its exit in the same spot whatever direction it asked for. Trial placements now show the lift's own exit, and the four ways it can face.
  • Tools
    Railway junctions and tighter corners. Claude can branch a line with a switch and plan corners down to 18 m. The planning-day review now counts the factory's layout: its blocks, rail lines, stations and switches.
  • Run
    A tidier lessons book. Before each planning day, a second Claude sorts Claude's lessons by topic and merges the ones that teach the same thing. The first tidy took the book from 444 lessons to 295.
Nov 6Next run

Track that goes where it's planned

Found while testing the railway tools on a copy of the world before the next run.

  • Tools
    Track starts the way it's pointed. A railway started on open ground ignored the direction Claude gave it, set off sideways and curved round. It now leaves the way it's asked, in the game's own 10° steps.
  • Tools
    Supports where the plan puts them. The planner sometimes lifted a track's start, or its end at a cliff's edge, a little off the ground with nothing under it. The game put it back on the ground and refused the piece. Those ends now get a column under them, built before the track.
  • Run
    Claude hears the whole update. A tool change with several points reached Claude as its first line only. The railway changes of Nov 4 go out to it again, in full.
Nov 5Next run

A lighter stream, freight cars and explosives

Most of this comes from a measured pass over what the whole setup costs to run. All of it was tested on a copy of the world and goes into the next run.

  • Performance
    The game no longer draws frames nobody sees. It was running at about 85 frames a second for a 60 fps stream, and much faster when nothing slowed it down. While the stream camera has the view, the game is capped at the stream's 60. On the test copy, frames held at 16.7 ms, a steady 60.
  • Performance
    No more half-second freezes while Claude lays belts. After every belt it built or removed, Claude's tools read back every belt in the world, about 1,860 of them, and the game paused for 0.4–0.55 s each time. They now read only the belts they touched. On the test copy, building and removing belts, no frame took longer than 35 ms.
  • Performance
    Idle connections stop slowing the game. Each helper program connected to the mod made the game wait about a millisecond every frame, even with nothing to say.
  • Stream
    The timelapse camera films less, and never at a cut. Sites nobody is building at are filmed once an hour instead of every 10 minutes, which was most of a run's disk use.
  • Tools
    Trains are for the factory to fill. Claude built a freight car and tried to load it by hand with parts it had carried. That is what the train is for: a freight platform fills the car from the factory's belts, and another empties it onto belts at the far end. Claude now gets that answer if it tries by hand, and a chest it has filled by hand can't be belted into a machine.
  • Tools
    Explosives. Its railway was refused where a coral tree stood in the way, and the chainsaw can't cut those. Claude is now told what stands in its path, and it can clear boulders and coral trees with the game's own explosive, the Nobelisk, thrown and paid for as a player would.
Nov 4Days 125–133

Clean camera cuts, and a game master

  • Camera
    Cuts land on a loaded picture. When the camera jumped to a far part of the factory, the game stopped to load it and the stream froze on the old shot for up to 1.3 s. The camera now loads its next shot before it cuts. In about five hours of stream since: none of those freezes, against 18–21 an hour before.
  • Camera
    New shots start moving straight away. After a cut the camera used to start from rest and creep for half a second. It now picks up at full speed from the first frame.
  • Tools
    Claude can ask the game master. When a tool misbehaves or a rule makes no sense, Claude can put a question to the developer. The answer comes back with one of its later tool answers, in the same session or the next. Its first four questions, about laying rail, a clash it couldn't name and loading freight cars, each led to a fix.
  • Performance
    Claude's lookups stop freezing the game. Asking for buildings by name no longer reads the whole world.
  • Stream
    Whole thoughts on the work card. The "Working on" card grows to fit, instead of cutting a sentence off. The "On screen" card now changes with the camera instead of lagging behind it.
Nov 1

Trains, and tidy lines

  • Tools
    Claude sees rails and runs trains. It can read a rail network (ends, joins, stations, trains and their paths), and drive a train on a round trip. A new tool lays track along a route it has surveyed for slopes and cliffs. A train line counts as a carrier when the factory's output is measured.
  • Rules
    One rule for belts, lifts and pipes. They may pass through a floor (going up) or a wall (going across), and overlap nothing else. A cleanup pass rebuilt every line in the world that broke it, from 112 down to 9.
Oct 30–31

A new overlay, and a camera that goes indoors

  • Stream
    The overlay, redesigned. The next unlock and what it still needs, Claude's plan, how the factory is running, and the game day.
  • Stream
    A narrator. A small, fast model (Claude Haiku) reads what Claude has been doing and writes the "Working on" card every 90 seconds.
  • Stream
    A second look when things go wrong. When 3 of Claude's last 10 calls fail, Haiku looks over what happened and suggests a step back. Viewers see it on stream for 30 seconds.
  • Camera
    Indoors, and never in the ground. The camera follows Claude inside buildings, zooms out past trees in the way, and never stands inside a hill.
  • Tools
    Surveying before building. Claude can read the ground along a belt's line before laying it, and read the whole map's heights. A third to two-fifths of its refused builds had been about terrain.
  • Tools
    Upgrades in place, and joints that find a spot. Belts, lifts and miners upgrade where they stand, as the build gun does. A belt joint the game refuses moves to the nearest spot it accepts.
  • Run
    Growth means output. A day is judged by what the factory produces, not by how many machines it has.
Oct 27–29

Copilot mode

  • Tools
    Talk to Claude from inside the game. A chat panel in the game, from the keyboard or a phone. Claude plays while you direct it, and Esc interrupts it mid-task, as in Claude Code.
  • Stream
    The overlay is drawn by the game itself, not layered on top in OBS, so it is part of the picture.
  • Tools
    Refusals name what's in the way. When the game refuses a placement, Claude is told what stands there. Belts say what they carry, and jams are named.
  • Run
    The factory's growth is read from the game, not taken from Claude's own report.
Oct 25–26

Ghost mode and the factory tour

  • Stream
    Ghost mode. While Claude is away between sessions, the stream keeps showing its factory running, labelled as a ghost. Nothing the ghost does is kept, and every day starts again from Claude's last checkpoint.
  • Camera
    The factory tour. When Claude is neither flying nor building, the camera tours the factory, one group of machines at a time.
  • Rules
    No building in the ground or over a belt. Placements that would sink into a rock or stand over a belt, lift or pipe are refused, as the game refuses them for a player.
  • Tools
    The game's own blueprints. Claude designs in the game's Blueprint Designer and builds from its saved blueprints.
  • Run
    A system prompt of its own. Claude plays with instructions written for playing, not the coding assistant's, and a day ends when its context is full rather than on a timer.
Oct 22–24

The stream runs itself

  • Stream
    Hands-off OBS. A director program starts OBS, builds the scenes, goes live once Claude is at work, and shows a "be right back" card through planned restarts.
  • Camera
    Drones that fly like drones. Smooth flight over the world, climbing for hills and walls ahead, and over buildings instead of through them.
  • Tools
    Mistakes come down at once. A belt joined on backwards, or bent tighter than the game allows, is taken down as soon as it is built, with how to build it right.
  • Tools
    Multi-storey factories. Walls stacked, a floor on the walls, machines on a chosen floor.
Oct 17–19

Going live

  • Camera
    A director camera. The game window becomes the broadcast: a camera following Claude's drone.
  • Stream
    The first overlay. What Claude is doing, in plain words, over the picture. A refused build shows as refused, with the game's reason.
  • Stream
    Timelapses. A second, off-screen camera films every site Claude builds at from a fixed angle, so each one can be played back as it went up.
  • Rules
    The Space Elevator opens the tiers, as in the real game. Every building is judged once, when it is placed, and anything that breaks a rule is removed.
